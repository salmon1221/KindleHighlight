> An alternative, internationally consistent standard implemented recently by the United Nations reveals an uncomfortable truth. The number of people in China’s towns and cities has already peaked (see chart 1). The urban population is projected to fall by more than 13m from 2024 to 2029. China’s urban future will be a combination of gloss, grime and decline. [↗](https://www.instapaper.com/read/1957752461/26385903)

> China’s urban decline could dent more than the country’s pride. It also threatens the ailing property market. Many observers were counting on further urbanisation to give the struggling industry a gentle, helping hand. [↗](https://www.instapaper.com/read/1957752461/26387131)

> Urbanisation, defined as the UN suggests, seems only loosely linked to economic development. National definitions often correlate better with GDP per person. That suggests China’s economy could still gain if urban folk move from towns to fuller-fledged cities. In these more bustling conurbations, they may reap more of the “agglomeration” benefits associated with cities, such as a finer division of labour, larger scale of operations and closer congregation of people to learn from. [↗](https://www.instapaper.com/read/1957752461/26387177)
